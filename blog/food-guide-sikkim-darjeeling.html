Food in Sikkim and Darjeeling: Vegetarian, Jain and Local Dishes

Local dishes worth trying, how hotels handle vegetarian and Jain requests, what to expect in the mountain villages, and what to pack from home.

By the Sikkim Tour and Travel team · Updated 12 October 2026

"Will we get our kind of food there?" is one of the first questions families ask us. It is a fair question, especially if you are travelling with parents who eat only home-style vegetarian food, or with fussy kids. The short answer: in Gangtok and Darjeeling, yes, comfortably. In the smaller mountain villages, food is simple but always manageable with a little planning.

Gangtok and Darjeeling: easy

Both towns get tourists from all over India and abroad, and their restaurants have adapted. You will find:

  • Restaurants serving most Indian cuisines – North Indian, South Indian, Bengali – mostly around MG Marg in Gangtok and around the Mall and Chowrasta in Darjeeling, plus cafés and bakeries.
  • Pure vegetarian restaurants – Gujarati, Marwari and North Indian – that are reliable for Jain and no onion-garlic meals.
  • Hotel kitchens that will make plain rice, dal, a dry sabzi and curd for dinner, and with a day's notice, poha, upma, parathas or other simple breakfasts.

The quality varies, of course – a dosa in Gangtok will not taste like one from your favourite place at home – but nobody goes hungry.

Pelling, Lachung, Lachen and the Silk Route: simple

These are small places where meals come from the hotel or homestay kitchen. Expect rice, dal, a potato or seasonal vegetable dish, roti, eggs and sometimes paneer. Maggi and tea are available everywhere. In North Sikkim, meals are usually included in your package because there are very few restaurants.

What helps:

  • We tell the hotel in advance about vegetarian, Jain or no onion-garlic needs. They cook a separate pot.
  • Simple comfort food is usually possible if you ask early – khichdi, dal-rice or curd rice; kitchens just need to know.
  • Our coordinator can speak to the cook on your behalf in Hindi or Nepali, if your explanation is getting lost.

Local food worth trying

If you are open to it, Sikkimese and Nepali food is mild, warming and often vegetarian-friendly:

  • Veg momos – steamed dumplings with cabbage, carrot or paneer, served with a spicy tomato chutney.
  • Thukpa – a noodle soup, perfect on a cold evening. Ask for the vegetarian version.
  • Nepali thali – rice, dal, greens, potato, pickle. Very close to a simple home meal.
  • Sel roti – a ring-shaped sweet rice bread, popular at festivals.
  • Darjeeling tea – try a first flush or second flush at a tea shop on the Mall. Drink it without milk to taste the difference.

Non-vegetarians will find chicken, pork and mutton everywhere. Seafood is limited this far inland.

Jain food

Jain meals (no root vegetables, no onion or garlic) are possible in Gangtok and Darjeeling, where there are pure-veg restaurants used to these requests. In smaller places, hotels can cook rice, dal and green vegetables without onion and garlic, but avoiding all root vegetables needs careful explanation. We brief every hotel in writing. Carrying some dry snacks and ready-to-eat Jain packs is a good backup.

What to pack from home

Experienced travellers with specific food needs pack a small "food kit". It takes less space than you think:

  • Your favourite pickle in a leak-proof bottle
  • Ready-to-eat meal packs for the North Sikkim and Silk Route days
  • Your usual coffee or tea, if you are particular about it
  • A spice mix you use at home – many hotel cooks are happy to use it
  • Dry snacks for kids: murukku, biscuits, dry fruits
  • A small electric kettle, if you want hot water any time for kids

Check your airline's rules on liquids for cabin bags; put pickles and powders in checked luggage.

Food for children and senior citizens

For small children, ask the hotel for plain rice with dal or curd, khichdi, or boiled vegetables – all easy to make. For seniors who need low-spice or diabetic-friendly food, tell us in advance; most hotels will adjust. Carry any regular medicines and some light snacks for long drives, since roadside options between towns are limited.

Water and hygiene

Drink only bottled or filtered water. Hotels will give you hot water on request – useful at high altitude where you should keep drinking even if you are not thirsty. Eat at busy places and avoid uncooked salads at roadside stalls.

How we handle food on our tours

Every package includes breakfast and dinner, and all meals in North Sikkim. When you book, we ask about food preferences for every person in the group and send them to each hotel. If something is not right on the day, call your coordinator and we fix it with the hotel directly. Food should be the last thing you worry about on holiday.

Quick answers

What local food should we try in Sikkim?

Momos, thukpa, Nepali thali, sel roti and Darjeeling tea are the easiest places to start, and vegetarian versions are widely available.

Is pure vegetarian food available in North Sikkim?

Yes, but simple: rice, dal, vegetables, roti. Tell us in advance and the hotel cooks separately for you.

Can hotels in Sikkim make Jain food?

In Gangtok and Darjeeling, yes. In smaller villages, no onion-garlic meals are possible; strict Jain requirements need advance briefing and some backup food.
