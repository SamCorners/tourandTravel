Gangtok & Sikkim Tour Package from Hyderabad

Whether you are flying from Shamshabad, or coming from Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada or Tirupati, we plan your Gangtok trip around the route that suits you best – with a Telugu-speaking coordinator on call and food that works for both vegetarian and non-vegetarian families.

Hyderabad families usually tell us two things on the first call: they want to get away from the April–May heat, and they want the trip to be comfortable for everyone from the grandparents to the kids. Gangtok is a good fit for both. It sits at about 5,400 ft, stays cool through summer, and has enough comfortable hotels and restaurants that nobody feels they are roughing it.

Getting from Hyderabad to Gangtok

Flying from Hyderabad (HYD)

Your destination airport is Bagdogra (IXB). From Hyderabad there is a direct flight on some days, taking roughly 2.5 hours; otherwise you connect through Kolkata, Delhi or Bengaluru, with a total journey of about 5 to 7 hours. Check current schedules when booking, as routes change by season. From Bagdogra, Gangtok is about 124 km and 4 to 5 hours by road along the Teesta river.

Our advice: pick a flight that lands at Bagdogra by early afternoon at the latest, so you reach Gangtok before dark. If your only option lands later, we start with Darjeeling (3 hours from the airport) and finish in Gangtok.

From Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada and the rest of Andhra Pradesh

Travellers from coastal Andhra have a useful advantage: Kolkata is close. From Visakhapatnam the flight to Kolkata is short, and Vizag also has good train links to Howrah. From Kolkata you can either connect by flight to Bagdogra or take an overnight train such as the Darjeeling Mail to New Jalpaiguri (NJP), arriving in the morning. We pick up from both Bagdogra and NJP at no extra cost. From Tirupati and Rayalaseema, flying via Hyderabad or Bengaluru is usually simplest.

How long should the trip be?

For a first Gangtok trip from Hyderabad we suggest 5 to 6 nights. Three nights in Gangtok gives you Tsomgo Lake, Baba Mandir and the city sights at an easy pace, and two nights in Darjeeling adds Tiger Hill and the toy train – that is our Gangtok & Darjeeling 5N/6D package. Families with summer holidays to spare usually take the Grand Sikkim 9N/10D tour, and groups who want snow and high lakes go for North Sikkim with Lachung and Lachen.

Telugu-speaking coordinator

Hotel staff and drivers in Sikkim speak Nepali, Hindi and English. Many of our Hyderabad guests are comfortable in Hindi, but grandparents often are not – and in an emergency, nobody wants to struggle with language. A Telugu-speaking coordinator is available on the phone throughout the trip, to explain the plan, talk to the hotel or the driver, and sort out anything that comes up.

Food for Telugu families – veg and non-veg

Hyderabad groups often include both vegetarians and keen non-vegetarians. Non-vegetarians do well in Sikkim: chicken, mutton, pork and fish are widely available, and the local momos, thukpa and Nepali-style meat curry are worth trying. Please do not expect Hyderabadi biryani – what you get in the hills is a pulao-style version.

For vegetarian families, every hotel serves dal, rice, roti and vegetables, and in Gangtok and Darjeeling you will find dosa and idli. We recommend carrying avakaya or gongura pickle, podi and a few ready-to-eat packs – especially for Lachung or the North Sikkim days, where meals are simple. We brief each hotel in advance on veg, Jain or no onion-garlic needs. See our full guide to South Indian food in Sikkim and Darjeeling.

For senior citizens and temple visits

Many Telugu families travel with parents who like to include temples and monasteries. Sikkim has some lovely ones: Baba Harbhajan Singh Mandir on the Tsomgo road, Ganesh Tok and Hanuman Tok in Gangtok, Rumtek and Enchey monasteries, and the Char Dham complex at Namchi, which has replicas of the four dhams and the twelve jyotirlingas with ramps and lifts for elderly visitors. Namchi is part of our Grand Sikkim tour and can be added to others. We keep altitude in mind for seniors: Gangtok and Darjeeling are comfortable for most, while Tsomgo is visited for an hour or so and Gurudongmar is only for the fit.

When to travel from Hyderabad

  • Late April to early June (school summer vacation): peak season. Hyderabad is above 40°C; Gangtok is around 15–22°C. Book early.
  • October and Dasara holidays: clear skies and the best mountain views – our top recommendation for photography.
  • Sankranti in January: cold, snowy and quiet. Great for snow lovers, but high roads like Nathula may close after heavy snowfall.

Month-by-month details are in best time to visit Sikkim, and documents for Tsomgo and North Sikkim are explained in our permit guide.

Frequently asked questions

How do I reach Gangtok from Hyderabad?

Fly from Hyderabad to Bagdogra (direct on some days, or via Kolkata or Delhi), then drive about 4–5 hours to Gangtok. We pick you up at Bagdogra airport at no extra cost.

Is a Telugu-speaking guide available?

A Telugu-speaking coordinator is on call through the trip to speak with hotels, drivers or doctors for you. Drivers speak Hindi, Nepali and basic English.

Will non-vegetarian food be available in Gangtok?

Yes. Chicken, mutton, pork and fish dishes are widely available, along with local momos and thukpa. Vegetarian and Jain food is also arranged.

Which Gangtok package is best for parents above 60?

The Gangtok & Darjeeling 5N/6D tour or the Grand Sikkim 9N/10D tour. Both keep overnight stays at comfortable heights and include a pilgrimage-friendly pace.
