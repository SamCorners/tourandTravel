Sikkim Permit Guide for Indian Tourists: Tsomgo, Nathula, North Sikkim and Zuluk

Which places in Sikkim need a permit, which documents to carry, what to do for children and how long it takes – in plain language.

By the Sikkim Tour and Travel team · Updated 12 October 2026

As an Indian citizen you do not need any permit to enter Sikkim itself, or to visit Gangtok, Pelling, Ravangla, Namchi or Darjeeling. But several of the most popular places are in border or protected areas, and these need a permit that is issued through a registered tour operator. Here is what you need to know.

Rules change from time to time. This guide reflects how permits generally work. We confirm the latest requirements for your dates when you book. Foreign nationals have separate rules and need a Restricted Area Permit; Nathula and Zuluk are not open to foreigners.

Documents you need

For each traveller, please send us scanned or clear phone-photo copies of:

  • A government photo ID: Aadhaar card, voter ID, passport or driving licence. PAN card is generally not accepted for these permits.
  • Passport-size photographs: 2 for Tsomgo/Nathula, and 2 to 4 for North Sikkim. Carry a few extra printed copies.
  • For children: a school ID card, birth certificate or Aadhaar. Infants need a birth certificate.

Carry the originals with you; checkposts can ask to see them. Make sure the name on the ID matches the name you give us.

How the process works with us

  1. Send documents on WhatsApp at least 5 days before arrival, for everyone in the group.
  2. We check them for clarity and matching names. Blurry or cut-off scans are the most common cause of delays.
  3. We apply through the registered-agent process required by Sikkim Tourism and the police.
  4. The driver carries the permit and handles the checkposts. You only show originals if asked.

For Tsomgo and Nathula, the application is made the day before your visit, which is why those excursions are always on your second day in Gangtok or later. For North Sikkim, permits are prepared once you are in Gangtok and you usually leave around 10 to 11 am.

Nathula Pass: extra things to know

  • Open to Indian tourists Wednesday to Sunday; closed Monday and Tuesday.
  • Daily permits are limited, so in peak season it can be fully booked. Tell us early if Nathula matters to you.
  • At about 14,140 ft, it is high. We do not recommend it for very young children, pregnant women, or anyone with heart, lung or blood pressure problems.
  • Photography of army installations is not allowed. Follow the instructions given at the pass.
  • Heavy snowfall closes the road at short notice. In that case the permit fee is usually refunded or adjusted as per the rules at the time.

North Sikkim: Lachung, Lachen and Gurudongmar

North Sikkim permits are only issued to travellers going with a registered operator in a Sikkim-registered vehicle. You cannot drive your own car or take a self-drive rental. The permit lists your exact route and dates, so changes on the day are not possible without a fresh permit.

Since the October 2023 Teesta flood, which damaged roads and the Chungthang area, the route to Lachen and Gurudongmar has seen long closures and phased reopenings. The Lachung side has generally reopened earlier. Permits are issued only when the administration declares the road open to tourists, so we always confirm before you book. Altitude: the other thing to prepare for

Permit areas are also the high-altitude areas. Some simple rules we give every guest:

  • Spend at least one night in Gangtok before going higher.
  • At Tsomgo, Nathula and Gurudongmar, walk slowly and do not run or climb stairs fast.
  • Drink water, avoid alcohol and smoking the night before.
  • Headache, breathlessness or nausea are signs to go down. Tell the driver immediately.
  • Ask your doctor about medication before the trip if you have any heart, lung or blood pressure condition. Do not take any altitude tablets without medical advice.
  • For Gurudongmar, carrying a small portable oxygen can is a good idea.

Common questions we get

Can I get the permit myself? For Tsomgo, Nathula and North Sikkim, permits are processed through registered operators, so individual travellers usually go through an agency. Do children need a permit? Yes, every person including infants is listed, which is why we need a document for them. Can one family member's ID cover everyone? No, each person needs their own ID.

Quick answers

Do Indians need a permit to visit Sikkim?

No permit is needed to enter Sikkim or visit Gangtok, Pelling and Namchi. Permits are needed for Tsomgo Lake, Nathula, North Sikkim and the Zuluk Silk Route.

Which documents are needed for the Nathula permit?

A government photo ID (Aadhaar, voter ID, passport or driving licence) and passport-size photos for each person. Children need a school ID or birth certificate.

On which days is Nathula open?

Nathula is generally open to Indian tourists from Wednesday to Sunday and closed on Monday and Tuesday. It also closes during heavy snowfall.

Is the PAN card accepted for Sikkim permits?

Generally no. Use Aadhaar, voter ID, passport or driving licence.
